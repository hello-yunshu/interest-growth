# Generic Research

Use when an interest question benefits from deliberate research rather than immediate advice.

1. Clarify the object, skill, concept, or decision being investigated.
2. Split the question into testable or checkable subquestions.
3. Prefer original/authoritative material appropriate to the domain, then useful syntheses and worked examples.
4. Record disagreements, failed cases, boundary conditions, and uncertainty.
5. Separate retrieved source material from model interpretation.
6. End with a small next observation, experiment, practice, or reading step.

Never claim to have read a source that was not actually retrieved. Do not force academic evidence hierarchies onto domains where practical demonstrations, standards, original works, or project artifacts are more appropriate.
